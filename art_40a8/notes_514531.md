## Setup
x⁴+y⁴+z⁴ = 51 t⁴. Fixing the ratio z/t = p/q (lowest terms) reduces the
question on that "fiber" to rational points on the plane quartic
C_M : X⁴+Y⁴ = M Z⁴,  M = 51q⁴ − p⁴  (genus 3).
C_M maps to the conic r²+s² = M u² and to the genus-1 quartic
D_M : a² = M − v⁴, whose Jacobian is E_M : y² = x³ + 4M x (CM by i;
PARI ellfromeqn). A point of C_M = a point of D_M whose a is a rational
square. This is the natural "search by fibers" organisation of the problem.

## Complete local analysis of D_M (new, validated)
Only p ≡ 3 (mod 4) and p = 2 can obstruct D_M locally (for p ≡ 1 mod 4
there are always points near v = ∞). With m = v_p(M), M' = M/p^m:
- p ≡ 3 (mod 4): m odd → insoluble; m ≡ 2 (mod 4) → soluble iff (M'|p)=1;
  m ≡ 0 (mod 4) → soluble iff (M'|p)=1 or M'−r⁴ is 0 or a QR mod p for
  some unit r.
- p = 2: soluble iff (m≡1 mod 4 ∧ M'≡1 mod 4) ∨ (m even ∧ M'≡1 mod 8) ∨
  (m≡2 mod 4 ∧ M'≡5 mod 8) ∨ (m≡0 mod 4 ∧ M'≡5 mod 16).
(Validated against brute-force ℚ_p solving on 6 primes × 400 M's, 0 errors.)

## Interpretation
No obstruction found anywhere: 51 is everywhere locally represented, the
Brauer–Manin computation reported in the question found no obstruction, and
the fiber analysis shows hundreds of everywhere-locally-soluble genus-1
quotients with provably infinite Jacobians. The failure is precisely the
square-lift: each rank-1 fiber offers a geometric progression of points
(heights roughly squaring rung to rung), and "a is a square" is a
codimension-½ event on each rung, so the expected number of hits per fiber
is a rapidly convergent sum ≈ Σ H_k^(−1/2) with H_k doubling in log —
a few percent per fiber, ~O(10) expected over the whole q ≤ 96 sky at all
heights. Consistent picture: solutions to 51 should exist but be very
sparse and very tall (the 17-country's guests at t = 583…2353, then a gap
to beyond 60,000, is the same statistics seen from the other side).
