# Notes toward an MO 513995 answer (Bring–Jerrard trigonometric parametrization)

Question: is R⁴ = sin ψ / sin 5ψ (with the d-formulas) documented?

Answer sketch (all verified here, `trinomial_verify.py`):

**Yes — the relation is classical in substance and explicit in recent
literature.**

- The R–ψ relation is the Im-part of d = x − x⁵ ∈ ℝ at x = Re^{iψ}:
  R sin ψ = R⁵ sin 5ψ. The d-formula is the Re-part after substitution:
  d = R sin 4ψ / sin 5ψ = sin^{1/4}ψ · sin 4ψ / sin^{5/4} 5ψ. The real-root
  hyperbolic form is the sinh⁵ multiple-angle identity. (Two-line derivations;
  60-digit certificates at the poster's own test values.)
- It generalizes verbatim: for xⁿ − x + d = 0, d real,
  R^{n−1} sin nψ = sin ψ and d = sin^{1/(n−1)}ψ · sin((n−1)ψ) / sin^{n/(n−1)} nψ
  (verified n = 3…12). Geometrically: for every real d, all non-real roots lie
  on the FIXED curve Im(x − xⁿ) = 0, independent of d.
- Literature: P. Bohl (1908) characterized the moduli of trinomial roots
  (the classical reference); Theobald & de Wolff, "Norms of roots of
  trinomials", Math. Ann. 366 (2016) re-derive the norm structure via amoebas
  (roots of given norm ↔ hypotrochoid curves; multiple roots at the
  singularities); and the sine-ratio form appears explicitly in
  Čermák, Fedorková, Jánský, "On moduli and arguments of roots of complex
  trinomials", Pacific J. Math. 332 (2024) — their central relation is
  precisely R^{n−1} sin nψ = sin ψ in our notation (for general trinomials
  with two free exponents).
- The collision values are the discriminant roots: for n=5,
  d* = (4/5)·5^{−1/4} = (256/3125)^{1/4}, double roots at x = ±5^{−1/4}.
