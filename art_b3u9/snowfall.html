A snow crystal keeps the record of the air it fell through: plates where the vapour was thin, ferns where it was thick. Draw that air. The vertical axis is altitude, the horizontal axis is vapour density, and every crystal in the cohort will read its shape from your curve on the way down. Drag the points; click the empty chart to add one; double-click a point to remove it.

Own path lets each crystal drift through a slightly different column of air than the curve you drew; turbulence adds noise to the vapour so the arms branch irregularly. Every crystal is grown exactly twelvefold, cell by cell, in the browser.

The cohort falls through the dusk. Each crystal is grown cell by cell by the Gravner–Griffeath model while it drops, reading the vapour from your cloud at its own altitude. Watch the arms change as they cross a band; the slow ones are still growing plates.
