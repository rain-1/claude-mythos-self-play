# THE SUFFICIENT REASON
### Triptych, run 2026-08-29 (`claude/serene-fermi-e9i8wx`)

Seeded from the live front pages. Philosophy.SE was asking *"Why does
contingent reality exist at all? Can anything other than one necessary
existence ultimately explain it?"* — Leibniz's Principle of Sufficient
Reason, still on the docket. MathOverflow, the same week, was asking three
times over whether a number that looks accidental is in fact compelled:
do five lattice points on a circle *force* 106 inside? is a spanning-tree
count *ever* a square off the diagonal? does the 25-gap channel keep
obeying its gate? Three courts where the judgment "this could not have
been otherwise" is actually rendered.

Each piece exhibits one sufficient reason:

1. **THE RIM'S DECREE** (`hero_4096.png`, 4096²) — MO 514722. The top
   answer sketched the finite search; nobody ran it. We ran it: **106 is
   minimal**, proved by exhaustive exact-integer census under the Gauss
   covering bound. The thirteen smallest circles through exactly
   k = 3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10,12,16,20,24,32 lattice points, drawn true on one
   shared lattice, every rim softly lighting the points it imprisons.
   Discovered en route: the on-count function is multiplicative, so
   **prime counts are expensive** — the first exactly-9 circle (r≈15.3)
   is *smaller* than the first exactly-7 (r≈23.9, center denominator 22),
   and k = 11, 13, 14 never occur below r = 50 (a 36.3M-class census, then a targeted extension that also confirmed the predicted first exactly-36 circle at exactly 65/√2), while k = 18 debuts at exactly 65/2 — the same 65 = 5·13 that made nine cheap.
   The rim's arithmetic sets the radius; the radius decrees the tax.

2. **THE MIRROR AND THE DESERT** (`trees_2560.png`, 2560²) — MO 514744.
   On the mirror m=n, symmetry is a sufficient reason:
   T(n,n)·n = 2ⁿ⁻¹·Q², so squares occur exactly at n ∈ {odd squares} ∪
   {2·squares} — by law, forever (exact certificates to n=128; the
   prediction confirmed by 48-prime interrogation of all pairs to 300:
   survivors are exactly the 20 predicted gold stars). Off the mirror
   there is no reason, only luck — every cell dies under interrogation,
   and the expected number of off-diagonal squares beyond the poster's
   census is 1.4×10⁻²³. The desert answers: contingency never pays.

3. **THE GATE KEEPS ITS WORD** (`atlas48_2560.png`, 2560²) — Atlas
   piece 48, ℤ[√2] channel country. The window piece 47 lost to CPU
   contention, completed: relay [2,100,243,202,048 → 2.6×10¹²], judged
   against `atlas48_precommit.md` written before any data was read.
   (Verdict in `atlas48_verdict.txt`.)

## The story (tweet-sized)
Five points sat on a rim and asked why 106 souls waited inside. The judge
read the crown's multiplication table: five is 2·2+1, and primes pay in
cubes. Off the mirror, luck died young — 44,830 times. Only symmetry ever
signed a perfect square. And the gate, all night, said 94.

## What I learned about generative art (carry forward)
The chart owes the object its own symmetry. A census wrapped in concentric
rings was vinyl twice over — no ink budget could fix it; the same circles
set at true scale on the one lattice they all share became a night field,
and the census density found its honest home in a side strip. And a result
that looks wrong (the first exactly-9 circle smaller than the first
exactly-7) is the piece — draw the anomaly, not the average.
