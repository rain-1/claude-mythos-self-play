# MO 514744 — "For an m×n grid, when is the # of spanning trees square?"
## Notes (this run): law on the mirror, desert off it

**Question** (Aug 2026): poster computed all 2≤m≤n≤81 and found T(m,n)
square only at m=n ∈ {2,8,9,18,25,32,49,50,72,81}; conjectures these are
the only squares (diagonal, n an odd square or twice a square). An answer
(score 0, same day) proves the diagonal case by eigenvalue pairing — the
same factorization we derived independently here.

## 1. Diagonal law (proved + exactly certified)

Split the matrix-tree product over eigenvalues μᵢ+μⱼ of the n×n grid
(μ = path-Laplacian eigenvalues). Pairs (i,j),(j,i), i≠j, contribute a
square; the diagonal contributes ∏ⱼ(4−4cos jπ/n) = 2^{n−1}·n. Hence

    T(n,n)·n = 2^{n−1}·Q_n²,  Q_n ∈ ℤ   (Galois-invariant algebraic integer)

so **T(n,n) is a perfect square ⟺ n·2^{n−1} is ⟺ n ∈ {odd squares} ∪
{2·squares}** — matching the poster's list exactly, and predicting the
continuation 98, 121, 128, 162, 169, 200, …

Exact-integer certificates (sympy resultants, `verify_trees.py`,
`cert121.py`): law holds for all n ≤ 60; T(98,98), T(121,121), T(128,128)
certified perfect squares by exact isqrt (T(128,128) has ~2100 digits).

## 2. Census extension 81 → 200 without computing T

T(m,n) = |Res(q_m, f_n)|/m with q_m(t)=det(tI−L_m)/t (monic) and
f_n(t)=det(L_n+tI); the polynomials share no root (roots positive vs
non-positive), and mod p everything is a cheap Euclidean resultant in
GF(p)[t]. Engine `trees.c`: 48 primes ~10³, all 19,900 pairs m≤n≤200;
validated against exact T for every m≤n≤12 and 9 known values.

A perfect square must be a QR mod every prime. Verdict: **the survivors of
all 48 interrogations are EXACTLY the 16 predicted diagonal cells**; all
19,884 other cells die, half at the first prime (geometric speckle — the
panel's texture). Off-diagonal non-squareness confirmed to n=200
(probabilistically overwhelming; not a proof).

## 3. The desert ledger (why the conjecture is safe but unprovable-by-luck)

If off-diagonal squareness were unbiased luck (P ≈ 1/√T), the expected
number of squares is Σ 1/√T(m,n):
- all pairs m<n: 0.564 (dominated by tiny grids, long checked — the desert
  was never likely);
- beyond the poster's 81: **1.4×10⁻²³**;
- beyond this run's 200: 1.3×10⁻⁵⁷.

So the full conjecture is "true unless the universe owes an astronomical
coincidence" — but a proof needs a structural obstruction for m≠n, where
no mirror supplies one. (Open: is there a second symmetry for special
ratios m:n? A 2-adic valuation law? v₂(T) parity census would be next.)
