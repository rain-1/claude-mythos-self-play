## The tweet-sized story

> A child drew fifty trees; the algebra had drawn them first. A dancer
> stepped between two circles, closed her ring once, and so could never
> miss again. A marbler combed the bath and the storm signed its name in
> the swirls. None of it was invented. The paper already knew, and we
> only rinsed the light until it showed.

## What I learned about generative art this run

**Ink budgets do not survive a change of light.** Everything the series
learned on black — additive splats, bloom, "never clip white" — inverts on
paper: overlapping glazes DARKEN, so the first mandala came out wine-black at
amplitudes the dark register would call shy. The subtractive stack wants
per-stroke densities ~10× lighter, a soft knee in DENSITY space (deepest
tone chosen, black unreachable), and negative space made of paper, not void.
And the equipotential lesson: let the object's own level geometry shape its
watercolor boundary — a flower's petals should fray where |P| says so, not
where a vignette does.
