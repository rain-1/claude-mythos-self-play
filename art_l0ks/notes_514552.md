# MO 514552 — "Literature and central asymptotics for a reciprocal-addition Pascal triangle"
## Answer-grade dossier (run 2026-08-23; POST decision open)

**The question.** A(0,0)=1; A(n+1,k) = A(n,k−1)⁻¹ + A(n,k)⁻¹ with out-of-row
entries 0 (so edges stay 1). Poster proves the Fibonacci first diagonal
(A(n,1) = F_{n+1}/F_n → φ) and fixed-diagonal convergence, observes numerically
A(n,⌊n/2⌋) − √2 ~ (−1)ⁿ·C/√n, C ≈ 0.05222, and asks (2) for a structural or
probabilistic explanation of the alternating n^(−1/2) decay.

**Answer in one paragraph.** Write the child map as the equal-parent involution
g(x) = 2/x perturbed by parent difference. Around the fixed point √2,
1/A = (1/√2)(1 − e + e² − …) with A = √2(1+e), so
e(n+1,k) = −(e(n,k−1)+e(n,k))/2 + O(e²): the linearization is MINUS the
Pascal averaging — the alternation is exactly g'(√2) = −1. In the sign gauge
d(n,k) = (−1)ⁿ(A(n,k)−√2) the interior evolves by plain binomial averaging,
which (i) makes the boundary data alternating, hence confined to a geometric
boundary layer, and (ii) CONSERVES the total deviation
M(n) = Σ_k d(n,k) up to boundary flux and O(d²) terms. The parity-averaged
mass therefore converges to a constant M̄, and the surviving bulk profile is
the fundamental solution of the averaging — the binomial kernel:

    A(n,k) − √2  ≈  (−1)ⁿ · M̄ · 2⁻ⁿ · C(n,k)        (bulk law)

whence the center value is (−1)ⁿ M̄ √(2/(πn)) and

    C = √(2/π) · M̄ ,   M̄ = 0.0654503304268973…  ⇒  C = 0.052221808…

**Certificates (all numeric checks in `tri_science.py`, `tri_precise.py`, `tri_checks.py`):**
- Bulk-law profile test at n = 32768: d(n, n/2+j) vs M̄√(2/πn)·e^(−2j²/n) agrees
  to ~4 significant digits at offsets 0, ¼√n, ½√n, √n, and even 2√n (value
  9.7e-8). The deviation field really is one Gaussian of conserved mass.
- C measured by √2-Richardson through n = 262144: limit 0.05222181 (remaining
  increments halve geometrically); prediction √(2/π)M̄ = 0.05222180814. Seven
  digits of agreement, and the poster's 0.05222 is the same constant.
- M̄ is parity-stable to ~1e-12 by n = 2.6e5.

**The boundary layer (poster's diagonals, completed).** The co-moving steady
state B_j (limit of the j-th diagonal) obeys the one-step recursion
B_j = g₊(1/B_{j−1}), g₊(u) = (u+√(u²+4))/2, B_0 = 1 — so B_1 = φ (the poster's
Fibonacci diagonal is the j=1 case), B_2 = (φ−1+√(6−φ))/2 = 1.35567…, and
linearizing gives B_j − √2 ~ K·(−1/3)^j (the −1/3 is (−1/B²)·g₊'(1/√2) =
(−1/2)(2/3)). Verified: empirical diagonals at n=32768 match B_j to 4e-16;
ratio −0.333333 by j=11. The row-sum parity split
M_odd − M_even = 1.0140041 equals the two-edge layer mass −4Σ_j(B_j−√2) =
1.0140074 to 3.3e-6 (the rest is the expected O(layer²) correction).

**About M̄.** It integrates the full nonlinear apex history, so we expect no
closed form; PSLQ vs {1, √2, φ, π, log 2, e, √π, γ, Σ(B_j−√2)} at the honest
11-digit level finds nothing. (Amusingly M̄ is within 4.8e-7 of π/48 —
five orders of magnitude outside our error bars. Not it.)

**Rigor status.** The mechanism (−1 multiplier ⇒ alternation; conservation ⇒
binomial spreading ⇒ n^(−1/2); C = √(2/π)M̄) is established at the level of a
careful linearization argument with numerically certified remainders; a full
proof needs (a) exponential attraction of the boundary layer (standard
contraction, ratio −1/3), (b) summability of the quadratic corrections to the
mass (numerically the increments decay fast), (c) a local CLT for the
inhomogeneous averaging. None looks hard; the statement C = √(2/π)·M̄ with M̄
defined as the parity-averaged limit of Σ_k(−1)ⁿ(A(n,k)−√2) is exact.

**Bonus corollary for question (1) (literature/normalization).** In the gauge
d the array is, to first order, the heat kernel on Pascal's triangle with
alternating Dirichlet data — which suggests searching literature under
"signed/alternating averaging schemes" rather than under reciprocal Pascal
arrays; we found nothing closer than the poster did.
