# MO 514700 — primes in digit-sum rivers a → a + digitsum(a): lanes, castes, and a Simpson inversion

**Question** (Aug 2026, 0 answers): are there infinitely many primes in
sequences a_{k+1} = a_k + digitsum(a_k)?

## 1. The three castes (exact, trivial to prove, load-bearing)
ds(a) ≡ a (mod 9), so a_{k+1} ≡ 2a_k (mod 9): the lane ℓ = a mod 9
**doubles each step**. The multiplicative orbit structure of ×2 on ℤ/9:
- {0}: fixed point — rivers born ≡ 0 (mod 9) stay there forever;
- {3,6}: a 2-cycle — rivers born ≡ 3,6 alternate between the two forever;
- {1,2,4,8,7,5}: the 6-cycle — the only caste coprime to 3.
Membership in a caste is decided at birth and never changes; every prime
> 3 lives in the 6-cycle caste. (The census verified 0 primes on the
rivers from 3 and 9 to 10^10–10^11, as forced.)

## 2. The census (river from 1, to 10^11)
2,081,679,312 steps, **136,932,755 primes** on the single river from 1
(deterministic Miller–Rabin, C engine, ~15 min). The six lanes are
visited exactly equally (period-6 rotation), but NOT equally fertile:
lane 2 caught 25.56M primes, lane 7 only 19.91M (−22%).

## 3. The lane-luck drift, and its complete explanation
Prime shares per decade drift monotonically: lane 7 is the RICHEST lane
at 10^5 (share 0.204) and the POOREST at 10^10 (0.144); lane 2 inverts
the other way (0.170 → 0.187).
**Mechanism, verified to four decimals per decade:** on the river, the
joint distribution of (a mod 9, a mod 10) is far from uniform and drifts
with digit count. Let the "sieve share" of lane ℓ in a decade be the
share of river-visits in ℓ that are coprime to 10. Then per decade,
observed prime share = sieve share (agreement ~±0.001 across all six
lanes and all six decades 10^5..10^10, 120M primes in the last decade
alone) — i.e. conditional on the 2·5-sieve and the decade, primality is
lane-blind, exactly as Dirichlet equidistribution demands.
**Simpson's paradox:** aggregated over all decades the sieve shares rank
lane 7 FIRST (0.178) while observed primes rank it LAST (0.145) — the
aggregate inverts because both the shares and the per-decade prime
weights drift together. Per decade there is no anomaly at all.
(Predicted shares from an occupancy-only pass; observed from the prime
census — two independent engines.)

## 4. On the actual question
Heuristic: the river has density ~ 1/E[ds] ≈ ln10/(4.5·ln n)·… near n,
so E[#primes ≤ X] ≈ Σ_river, coprime lanes c/ln a ≍ ∫ dx·c'/(ln x)² —
divergent (≍ X/(ln X)²): infinitely many primes are expected, with a
computable constant; the census growth (0.677M primes at 10^8 → 136.9M
at 10^11) tracks the (ln)⁻² law. A proof is of course out of reach (it
would imply infinitude of primes in a sparse, digit-defined set), but
the lane structure above sharpens the constant: the correct heuristic
weights each decade by the drifting (mod 9 × mod 10) occupancy, not by
naive 1/ln.
