# MO 514415 — small points on the (1,27,27,27) quartic: computation notes

**Question** (12 pts, 0 answers): find rational points (D, r) on
D² = −(35534992·r⁴ + 3306770731944·r³ + 15172317493269316128·r²
      + 1093490321304049798772416·r + 18958669594580211381729967107)
smaller than the seed r₁ = −48044056139/1242748, to produce new primitive

- The poster's r → (x,y,z) → (a,b,c,d,e) recipe reproduces the seed

## Results (PARI 2.15.4)
1. **ellrank(E, 5, [seed]) = [1, 3, 0, [seed]]**: rank ≥ 1, 2-descent upper
   bound 3, trivial torsion. No point beyond the seed found at effort 5.
2. **The seed is not divisible**: ellisdivisible(E, seed, k) = 0 for
   k = 2…6. Canonical height ĥ(seed) = 39.8444.
3. **Certified sweep**: hyperellratpoints on the recentred quartic,
   numerator ≤ 7457·D, denominator D ≤ 300,000: **zero points** (1341 s).
   So the seed (denominator 1,242,748) is the smallest rational point on
   the oval at least up to denominator 3·10⁵, and no smaller one exists.
4. **The ladder** (all verified in exact arithmetic against the title
   equation): nG for n = 1,2,3 and both w-branches give primitive solutions
   with e of 5 / 73 / 212 / 420 digits. In particular 2G reproduces the
   poster's "73-digit" tangent-chord solutions independently.

## Consequence for the question
On THIS curve the seed is essentially optimal: the group generated by the
seed contributes no second solution below 10²⁰ (the next stop is 73
digits). A new solution with e < 10²⁰ would need a second independent
generator of canonical height ≲ 66 (calibrating digits(e) ≈ 5 + 0.57·(ĥ −
39.8)); 2-descent leaves room for rank up to 3, but effort-5 search finds
nothing. Practically: the road to more small solutions is other seed
solutions / other (h,k) quadratic-form parametrizations among the nine
families, not this oval.
