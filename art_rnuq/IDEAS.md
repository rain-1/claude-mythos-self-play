The thread that runs through the philosophy page is *saying*: what a yes/no answer commits you to, whether a
truth can be told in any order, what can be said consistently at all. The mathematics of that is discrepancy
theory (a territory this series had never touched in 70 runs) and the Matula tree of a number.
Triptych title: **WHAT CAN BE SAID**.

## Six ideas

1. **The Longest Honest Answer** — *Erdős discrepancy.* A ±1 sequence of length 1160 whose every homogeneous
   ledger x_d + x_{2d} + … stays within ±2 (found here by SAT, Konev–Lisitsa's record; 1161 is impossible, and
   Tao proved no infinite sequence keeps any bound). Drawn as a rose: ring d tiled by its ledger cells, warm
   for +, cool for −, paper for zero; pigment family drifts with depth; a coral seam where the 1,161st answer
   would go. **BUILT (hero, 4096²).**
2. **The Meaning of a Number Is a Tree** — *Matula–Goebel.* 1 is a leaf; n = p_k·p_l·… is a root whose branches
   are the trees of k, l, …; every rooted tree appears exactly once. The first 120 numbers planted as a garden,
   blossoms tinted by depth, coral stalks for the bamboo numbers 1, 2, 3, 5, 11, 31, 127. **BUILT (2560²).**
3. **The Order You Tell It In** — *Steinitz / Banaszczyk.* The same 720 vectors summing to zero, told in
   different orders: a circle, roses of m circles, a Brownian loop, and the balanced order that never leaves
   the coral circle of radius √5/2 (Banaszczyk 1987, sharp in the plane). **BUILT (2560²).**
4. **Scattered Fair** — the local discrepancy function D(x,y) = #points in [0,x)×[0,y) − Nxy of the Fibonacci
   lattice beside random points (prototyped in grey: tweed vs Brownian sheet). Dropped: too close in register
   to the 09-13 hyperuniform piece.
5. **Thirty-Three Questions Without an Answer** — Peres' Kochen–Specker set: 33 directions in space whose
   orthogonal triads admit no consistent yes/no assignment (the Barber's paradox as physics), as a sphere
   mandala of great-circle arcs.
6. **The Sky of Simple Groups** — MO 199874: every finite simple group of order below 10³⁰ as a bead on a
   log axis, the 18 families as threads, the 26 sporadic groups in coral.
