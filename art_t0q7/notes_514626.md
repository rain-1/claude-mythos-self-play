# MO 514626 — Manhattan distance matrix of a permutation — evidence dossier

**Conjecture (poster).** For D_π = |i−j| + |π(i)−π(j)|:
|det D_π| ≥ (n−1)·4^{n−1} for every π ∈ S_n.

Note D_π = A + ΠAΠᵀ with A = (|i−j|) the path distance matrix,
det A = (−1)^{n−1}(n−1)2^{n−2} (Graham–Pollak), so the floor is exactly
|det(2A)| and is attained by π = id and π = reversal (both give D = 2A).

## Census verdicts (exhaustive)

| n | perms | violations | # attaining floor |
|---|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 2..9 | all | 0 | exactly 2 (id, reversal) |
| 10 | 3,628,800 | 0 | 2 |
| 11 | 39,916,800 | 0 | 2 |

Total 43,589,145 permutations, zero below the floor; equality **only** at
id and reversal (n ≥ 3... n=2: id=reversal's D coincide up to relabel, 2 perms).

**Quantized bottom rungs (new observation).** For 4 ≤ n ≤ 10 the
*second-smallest* value is exactly

    floor + (n−2)·4^{n−2}   (attained e.g. by the adjacent transposition at the boundary),

and the third-smallest is floor + 2·(n−2)·4^{n−2}. The bottom of the
spectrum is a discrete ladder in units u = (n−2)4^{n−2} before a continuum
sets in (values floor + 2.2083u, 2.3333u, ... appear).

**Divisibility.** gcd of all |det D_π| over S_n (exhaustive): 4³, 4³, 4⁴,
4⁵, 4⁵ for n = 7, 8, 9, 10, 11; sampled multiples suggest 4⁵ at n=12 and
4⁷ at n=16. Odd n = 4^{(n−1)/2} fits 7, 9, 11; even n does not follow the
same rule (n=8 gives 4³, n=10 gives 4⁵). Exponent law open.

## Structure: the pencil of two Minkowski forms

Let μ_1..μ_n be the generalized eigenvalues of the pencil (A, B),
B = ΠAΠᵀ (roots of det(A − μB) = 0). Then **exactly**

    |det D_π| = |det A| · ∏|1 + μ_i|,     ∏ μ_i = det A / det B = 1.

Both A and B are conditionally negative definite (L1/cut metrics): negative
definite on the common hyperplane 1⊥, one positive Perron direction —
"two Minkowski spacetimes sharing a simultaneity slice." The conjecture is
a **reverse Minkowski determinant inequality**: ∏|1+μ_i| ≥ 2^n given ∏μ_i = 1.

- If all μ_i were real > 0 this follows from AM–GM
  (∏(1+μ_i)^{1/n} ≥ 1 + (∏μ_i)^{1/n} = 2). But the pencil is NOT always
  real: at n=10, 13.1% of eigenvalues are complex (over 400k random π).
- The general Lorentzian statement is **false**: random pairs of signature
  (1, n−1) matrices sharing a timelike vector, and even sharing the whole
  negative hyperplane, violate reality ~50% of the time and can violate
  the determinant bound. The permutation structure is essential.

## New sharp conjectures (observed laws)

Over 400,000 random π at n=10 (4,000,000 eigenvalues) and exhaustive n ≤ 9:

1. **Sector law.** Every pencil eigenvalue satisfies Re μ > 0; in fact
   |arg μ| < π/3, and the constant π/3 is sharp:
   the "half-reversal" permutations σ = (h−1,...,0, n−1,...,h) have
   max |arg μ| ↑ 60°: 56.25° (n=10), 58.93° (32), 59.74° (128), 59.87° (256),
   with 60° − max ≈ c/n (log–log slope −1.03). Exhaustive maxima over ALL
   of S_n for n ≤ 9 are attained by (near-)half-reversals: 48.78°, 53.13°
   (= arctan 4/3), 53.30°, 55.15°, 55.22° — all < 60°.
2. **Asymptotic reality.** max |arg μ| over random π falls like ~1/n
   (37.7° at n=12, 3.3° at n=96): for large n a typical pencil is nearly real,
   so the AM–GM mechanism "almost" applies.
3. **Unit-circle avoidance.** Complex eigenvalues cluster near |μ| = 1 but
   visibly avoid the circle itself (dark seam in the density field).
4. min ∏(1+μ_i) over each exhaustive S_n equals 2^n exactly (the identity).

These reduce the conjecture to: *the complex pairs never conspire to beat
the real AM–GM slack*. Note the cone alone is NOT enough per pair: at the
cone edge a unit-circle pair gives |1+μ|² = 2+2cos60° = 3 < 4, short of
the AM–GM pair target — the deficit must be repaid by the real part of the
spectrum, so any proof has to treat the spectrum globally (e.g. via the
reciprocal pairing between π and π⁻¹'s pencils). Open, with a precise
geometric target.

Failed routes recorded: q-monotonicity of the border term (false);
reverse Minkowski via Gårding hyperbolicity (det is not hyperbolic on
signature (1,n−1)); naive Jensen on the disk |s−1| ≤ 1 (gives positivity,
not the floor). Positivity itself (det D_π ≠ 0, sign (−1)^{n−1}, one
positive eigenvalue) follows from strict negative type of L1 metrics on
distinct points.
