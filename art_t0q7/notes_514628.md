# MO 514628 — area of a union of parallelograms of a permutation — experimental dossier

**Problem** (Kaczynski 1998 → Kuklinski arXiv:2311.16210 → MO 514628).
Split bottom and top of the unit square into n slots; σ ∈ S_n joins bottom
slot i to top slot σ(i) by a parallelogram of area 1/n. T_σ = union;
α_n = min_σ area(T_σ). Known: α_n ≪ (log n)^{−p} → 0; conjectured order
1/log n; asked: α_n ≤ C/log n?

At height y the n panes are unit-width (1/n) intervals with left ends
(i−1 + y·(σ(i)−i))/n; area(T_σ) = ∫₀¹ |union| dy — piecewise linear in y
with O(n²) kinks, so exact evaluation is cheap (`cloth_lib.py`,
`area_exact`, validated against an 8192-slab midpoint rule to 7e−7).

## New results (this run)

1. **Exact minima for n ≤ 9** (exhaustive over S_n with exact areas):
   the *full reversal* is the unique-up-to-symmetry minimizer and

       α_n = (n+1)/(2n)   for 2 ≤ n ≤ 9.

2. **The reversal is dethroned exactly at n = 10**:
   σ = [9 8 7 4 3 6 5 2 1 0] (reversal with its central four entries
   re-braided) has exact area 329/600 < 11/20 = reversal. (Found by
   annealing; area exact-verified.) So the crossover from "one pinch"
   to structured overlap happens at n = 10.

3. **The hourglass waist.** Simulated annealing (segment-reversal /
   swap / block-rotate moves, grid objective, exact refine) discovers, at
   every n ≥ 10, cloths that hold the union NARROW over a whole band of
   heights instead of pinching once:
   best found: 0.5483 (n=10), 0.4954 (20), 0.4651 (32), 0.4448 (48),
   0.4340 (64), 0.4215 (128), 0.4078 (256), 0.4161 (512, under-annealed).
   Union-length profile of champions: falls from 1 at y=0 to a ~0.24
   plateau across y ∈ [0.38, 0.62], then back — an hourglass rope, the
   discrete embryo of the Perron-tree mechanism.

4. **Engineered families** (for scale): forced-waist designs (all lines
   through a staggered window of width W at heights y ∈ [0.4, 0.6]) reach
   0.458 (n=256) → 0.434 (n=2048); nested reversal cascades (global +
   scale-8 + scale-64) are comparable. Both plateau — single-scale wastes
   the logarithm; the descent to 0 needs waists-within-waists (slope
   groups), consistent with Kuklinski's construction.

5. For reference: identity = 1; random σ ≈ 0.5954 (n=4096; → 1−1/e-ish
   mid-heights); plain bit-reversal is *bad* (0.601 at 4096): multiscale
   in the wrong coordinates.
