# Stick broken in 5 places: can the 6 pieces be the edges of a tetrahedron?

## Method
- `tetra.c`, a single-threaded C program with the xoshiro256** RNG. It takes 5 iid U(0,1) cut points, sorts them, and uses the 6 spacings as the pieces.
- The 6! edge assignments reduce to **30 classes** under the 24 vertex symmetries. They are generated by canonicalising every permutation; `./tetra list` prints them. Edge order is 01,02,03,12,13,23, and pieces are given by rank (0 = smallest).
- Validity test for one assignment: all 4 faces must be strictly triangular, and the Gram determinant
  det G > 0, with G_ij = (d0i^2 + d0j^2 - dij^2)/2 (det G = 36 V^2 = CM/8). Face 012 strict plus det G > 0 makes G positive definite, so this criterion is exact.
- A necessary prefilter speeds things up: the two faces on the longest edge need min(s4+s1, s3+s2) > s5 (sorted pieces).
- Independent check (`verify.py`): a numpy 5x5 Cayley–Menger determinant over all 720 permutations. Across 4000 dumped samples it gave 0 mismatches in validity, nvalid, best-volume assignment and volume.
- Speed: about 7.5e6 samples/s on one core.

## Main estimate
4.0e9 samples (20 seeds x 2e8):

**P = 0.0652821, s.e. 3.9e-6, 95% CI [0.065275, 0.065290].**

Distribution of the number of valid classes (out of 30) among successes (mean 5.78):

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 12 | 24 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| frac | .1871 | .2328 | .0647 | .1002 | .0335 | .1668 | .0162 | .0287 | .0205 | .0199 | .0216 |

Every n from 1 to 30 occurs. Even values, multiples of 6 and n=24 are favoured. The full table is in `runs/summary.txt`.

## Variants
- **(a) Normalised iid Exp(1):** P = 0.0652697 ± 1.24e-5 (4e8 samples). This is within 0.95 sigma of the uniform result, as expected: the spacings are exactly Dirichlet(1,...,1), which is the law of normalised exponentials.
- **(b) All 30 classes work:** P(all 30 | at least 1) = **0.02157**, and unconditionally P(all 30) = 0.001408. The ratio max/min ≤ √2 is sufficient for this (P ≈ 7.5e-5) but far from necessary. I found all-720-valid samples with max/min up to 9.65; for example (.1466,.0969,.1713,.2065,.1973,.1814) has ratio 2.13 and was checked independently. So the √2 "bound" quoted on MSE is a strict lower bound only.
- **(c) Canonical assignment conjecture: FALSE.**
  - The rule "largest opposite smallest, 2nd opposite 2nd smallest, 3rd opposite 3rd" fixes the opposite pairs (6,1),(5,2),(4,3), where 1 = smallest. That leaves two classes:
    - The 3 largest at a common vertex (class 6). It fails in 41.7% of successes.
    - The 3 largest forming a face (class 0). It fails in 83.2% of successes, and it is never needed when class 6 fails.
  - The best single class is class 12. Its opposite pairs are (1,6),(2,4),(3,5) and its faces are {1,2,3},{1,4,5},{2,5,6},{3,4,6}. It works in only 75.8% of successes.
  - **No single canonical assignment exists.** Using bit-masks of the valid classes from 3e8 samples (1758 distinct masks), each of these 7 classes is, for some samples, the *only* valid one:

    | class | edges 01,02,03,12,13,23 (1 = smallest) | greedy share |
    |---|---|---|
    | 12 | 1,2,5,3,4,6 | 0.7582 |
    | 13 | 1,2,5,3,6,4 | 0.1525 |
    | 26 | 1,3,5,4,6,2 | 0.0816 |
    | 14 | 1,2,5,4,3,6 | 0.0040 |
    | 15 | 1,2,5,4,6,3 | 0.0025 |
    | 6 | 1,2,4,3,5,6 | 0.0012 |
    | 7 | 1,2,4,3,6,5 | 1.8e-5 |

  - The minimum hitting set is exactly these 7 classes (it is unique). In the sample they cover every success.
  - **Conjecture:** if any assignment works, one of these 7 works. The other 23 classes are never needed.
  - Counterexamples, each with exactly one valid class (verified over all 720 permutations; `runs/single.txt`):
    - only class 12: (.02488,.14545,.15573,.18522,.20283,.28589). This also refutes both versions of the largest-opposite-smallest rule.
    - only class 7: (.01634,.16997,.18540,.20820,.20934,.21074)

## Literature
- MSE 351913 / MO 142983. leonbloy (MSE) got p ≈ 0.06528 from 1e9 samples. He also found that the same 7 permutations suffice, with greedy shares 0.758/0.152/0.082/0.004/0.0025/0.0012/0.00002, which match the table above. Sasha (2013) gave ≈ 0.0652823. A recent MO answer reports 0.0652818 ± 4e-7 from randomised QMC.
- The estimate here, 0.0652821 ± 3.9e-6, agrees with all of these.
- The Illinois Geometry Lab report (May 2015, Hildebrand) could not be downloaded (connection reset). The MO question says its main numerical finding agrees with the MSE estimate.
- No closed form is known.

## Files
- `tetra.c` has these modes: `stats N seed [exp]`, `dump NS NF seed`, `masks N seed`, `single N seed`, `list`.
- Scripts: `aggregate.py`, `analyze_masks.py`, `verify.py`.
- Raw outputs are in `runs/`.
- `successes.json` has 2000 objects of the form {pieces, assign (piece index per edge 01..23), nvalid, volume}, using the maximum-volume assignment. `failures.json` has 2000 {pieces}.
