# MO 514772 — "How many lattice points can lie on a circle with exactly n interior points?"
## Answer-grade notes (exhaustive census + structure, this run)

**Question** (Aug 2026, score 7): f(n) = max lattice points on a circle whose
interior holds exactly n lattice points. Poster proves f(n) ≤ ⌊2+√(8n+4)⌋
(parity/midpoint argument), shows tightness at n=0 (f=4) and n=4 (f=8), asks
whether the bound can be substantially improved. The posted answer (score 10)
gives the asymptotic shape: f(n) ≤ n^{(3log2+o(1))/loglog n}, with a lower
bound of exponent log 2 from circles x²+y²=r², so f(n) = n^{Θ(1)/loglog n};
the constant is open between log 2 and 3 log 2.

## 1. What we computed (new, not in the thread)

Exhaustive census of ALL circles through ≥3 lattice points with exactly
n ≤ 8192 interior points. Correctness: such a circle has
r ≤ √(n/π) + 1/√2 (closed unit squares centered at interior points cover
D(c, r−1/√2)), so RMAX = 51.75 covers n ≤ 8192. Engine `fcensus.c`:
translation-class dedupe by the invariant key (A, −G mod 2A, −F mod 2A,
G²+F²); rim count recovered from hash multiplicity k(k−1)(k−2)/2 and
ASSERTED against a direct count on every k≥4 class (1.49M asserts, 0
failures); exact integer interior counts. Validated against an independent
Fraction-arithmetic brute force (n ≤ 20, exact agreement) and against the
poster's two anchors. The NMAX=4096 and NMAX=8192 runs (different RMAX,
different hash loads) agree on every shared value.

### Exact values — the record ladder
f is NOT monotone; the running maximum is set at exactly seven records:

| n | f(n) | witness center | 4r² = 2·N | N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | (1/2,1/2) | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 8 | (3/2,1/2) | 10 | 5 |
| 32 | 12 | (7/2,1/2) | 50 | 25 |
| 96 | 16 | (11/2,3/2) | 130 | 65 = 5·13 |
| 500 | 24 | (25/2,5/2) | 650 | 325 = 5²·13 |
| 1716 | 32 | (47/2,1/2) | 2210 | 1105 = 5·13·17 |
| 6624 | 36 | (91/2,13/2) | 8450 | 4225 = 5²·13² |

**Every record is a half-integer-center circle** whose 4r² = 2N climbs the
split-prime ladder: N = the smallest integers (products of primes ≡ 1 mod 4)
maximizing r₂. Rim count = #{u²+v² = 2N, u,v odd} = r₂(N) = 4·∏(aᵢ+1); all
representations of 2N (N odd) are odd-odd, so the full divisor wealth is
spent on the rim. Record rims are exactly 4·(max ∏(aᵢ+1)) — never the
exotic-denominator classes (which dominate the *minimum-interior-for-odd-rim*
question, cf. MO 514722 notes): **evidence that the true constant in
n^{c/loglog n} is log 2, the answer's lower bound, not its upper 3 log 2.**

### The parity bound is tight ONLY at n = 0 and n = 4 (all n ≤ 8192)
The poster's two examples are the only tight cases in range. Conjecture: the
only tight cases, period. (Provable in principle: f(n) = n^{o(1)} ≪ √(8n)
forces finitely many; our census pushes the frontier to 8192.)

### n = 6 is a VOID (new observation)
No circle through ≥3 lattice points has exactly 6 interior points — the
unique such n ≤ 8192. (f(6) = 2, and the witness is poetic: perturb the n=4
record circle (2x−1)²+(2y−3)²=10 by moving its center to (0.499, 3/2) — six
of its eight rim points fall away, two of them fall INSIDE, and the circle
through (0,0),(0,3) now holds exactly 6 with 2 on the rim. The void at 6 is
the record at 4, nudged.) Proof is the finite census: r ≤ 2.09
allows only a handful of classes; none lands on 6. Conjecture: 6 is the only
void for all n.

### f(n) = 3 stragglers (all of them, n ≤ 8192)
n ∈ {3, 7, 15, 17, 18, 23, 51, 63, 65, 103, 141, 147, 307, 883} — fourteen
values where no 4-point circle achieves exactly n. All but 18 are odd (odd
interior counts repel symmetric centers); witnesses live at exotic center
denominators 6, 10, 26, 30, 58, 62, 74, 82, 122, 146, 148. After 883:
every n ≤ 8192 has f(n) ≥ 4.

### Predicted-then-confirmed
Precommitted before the NMAX=8192 run (f_precommit.md): exactly one new
record, f=36 near n≈6630 from the 5²·13² tower rung; void stays {6}; no new
stragglers; no new tight n. All confirmed: f(6624)=36, r=65/√2 — the same
circle the 08-29 census found as ρ(36) with interior 6624 (independent
engine, exact agreement). Honesty note: the precommit wrote the witness
equation with the wrong normalization (4r²=4225 instead of 8450 — an odd
number is not a sum of two odd squares); the n-band and rim were right.

### Beyond the shore (exact direct counts, lower bounds only)

- f(50304) ≥ 64: circle 4r² = 64090 = 2·5·13·17·29.

with more RAM/time).
